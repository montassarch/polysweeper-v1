## Research (ps-researcher, 09:00) (partial)
**Saved at 09:10 UTC; final version follows.** Question 1 (row 4): can a bot read a rule LIVE that removes the loser fills? Tested on 13 months (Sep 2025 to Oct 2026), not 32 days. Question 2 (new, from the data): where is the volume for such bids? Scripts: `lab/row4_history.py`, `row4_rules.py`, `row4_diag.py`, `row4_pegup.py`, `row4_summary.py`, `tennis_decided_bids.py`, `espn_clock_poller.py`; results `lab/results/2026-10-09-*.json`.

**A. Row 4, long history (6,973 US games listed, 6,285 with a resting-bid fill at 0.98+ in the last 30 min before Polymarket's stamp; 198k fills; ESPN play-by-play matched on 98.7%).**
- A resting buy at 0.98+ that gets filled in the last 30 minutes of a US game lands on the loser in **37 of 6,285 games (0.6%)**: MLB 11/2,564, NBA 7/1,182, NHL 7/1,263, CFB 5/535, WNBA 5/366, NFL 2/375. In 248 of 259 loser fills (96%) the bought side was still AHEAD at the fill (the flip came later), so the "cancel-follow shield" (idea 26) could remove at most 4% of them: **rejected**.
- The earlier "0 losers in the last 12 minutes before the stamp" (32 days) does **not** survive 13 months: 10+ loser games have fills inside the last 12 min, 3 inside the last 5 min (Western Michigan at Michigan, 0.999, lead 5 with 7 s left).
- Clock-only rules still lose games (loser games / games, bought side ahead): last 60 s: CFB 1/353, NFL 1/327, NBA 5/1,126, WNBA 3/313; "5 s or less": NFL 1/308 (Denver-Indianapolis, lead 2 at 3 s, last-second field goal), NBA 1/1,019 (3-point lead, 2.8 s, OT), WNBA 1/231. MLB: 9th inning or later, lead 4+: 2/730 lost. Hockey cannot be read from the clock history (ESPN's NHL clock counts UP; 4 of 7 hockey losers are 'tied in overtime' labels where the lead was blown with the goalie pulled): no NHL clock rule is trustworthy.
- **Rules with 0 losers in hundreds of games:** NBA last 60 s and lead 5+: **0/953 games** (95% bound 0.3%); NBA last 2 min and lead 9+: 0/796; NFL last 60 s and lead 3+: 0/293; NHL last 60 s and lead 2+: 0/672 (labels shaky); and **"game over"** (final horn / final out / walk-off): MLB 0/1,474, NBA 0/997, NFL 0/308, CFB 0/291, WNBA 0/226, NHL 0/1,077 = **0 of about 4,400 game-instances**. Reading the state 45 s late changes nothing about the zeros.
- Cheap supply (taker sells into bids at 0.98-0.995, 5+ shares) inside those rules, per active day of that sport: NBA lead 5+ last minute 1.8 games (43 fills); NFL lead 3+ last minute 1.2 games; NHL lead 2+ last minute 1.3 games; game over: MLB 3.2 games, NHL 2.9, CFB/NFL 1.5, NBA/WNBA 1.2. With a 45 s reading delay these fall by 30-60%. Median shares sold per qualifying game: 200 (MLB) to 3,600 (NBA).
- A bid left resting under a rule fills later when the state is worse: without cancelling, NBA "last minute lead 5+" shows 5 loser games in 381 filled; cancelling within 45 s of the rule failing leaves 1 in 316. So the live rule needs cancel logic, not only entry logic.
- **Projection to 21 Oct:** Polymarket already lists CFB 221 games (93 on Sat 10 Oct, 99 on Sat 17 Oct), NHL 88, NFL 28, NBA 31 (preseason), MLB 6. That is about 90-120 qualifying game-instances and 30-60 pretend fills at best (queue unknown) against 200 needed. **Row 4 cannot become YES by 21 Oct**; it stays NOT YET with a safe-looking rule.
- ESPN's play stamps can be 100 s or more off (hockey overtime goal at -526 s while the market still showed 0.91 at -440 s), so clock rules should be judged on the live poller (`lab/espn_clock_poller.py`), not on this history. The "game over" zeros are robust to this because mislabelling can only add risk.
