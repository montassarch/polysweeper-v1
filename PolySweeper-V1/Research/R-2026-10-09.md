## Research (ps-researcher, 09:00) (partial)
**Work in progress at 09:00 UTC (saved early on purpose).** Question: row 4, find a rule that a bot can read LIVE (game clock, lead, ball, inning, outs) and that removes the loser fills, tested on 13 months instead of 32 days.
- Built `lab/row4_history.py` (events, public trades of each game's last 30 min, ESPN play-by-play) and `lab/row4_rules.py` (gates, loser counts per fill and per game, 95% upper bounds). Games listed: 6,973 with one resolved moneyline (MLB 2,701, NHL 1,336, NBA 1,188, CFB 990, NFL 377, WNBA 381; Sep 2025 to Oct 2026). Results follow in the final section.
- Three data traps found and fixed on the way: (1) ESPN's MLB rows carry the score and outs AFTER the whole at-bat on every pitch row, so a state read mid-at-bat leaks the result (the 32-day MLB "inning/lead" gates were slightly leaky); (2) NFL ties pay 50/50 (3 of 399 NFL games, one regular-season 40-40) and earlier studies dropped them: a bid on the side that is ahead loses 0.48 a share there; (3) Gamma's NFL/CFB series ids change each season (12185/12756 are 2026; 10187/10210 are 2025).
- Capital lock (new, from 6,000 games): a market closes a median 76 min (MLB), 111 (WNBA), 128 (CFB), 138 (NHL), 147 (NFL), 232 min (NBA) after the finished stamp; the slowest tenth takes 5 h (MLB) to 38 h (CFB). With a $20 test, one 5-share bid at 0.99 ($4.95) ties up a quarter of the money for about 2 hours.
- New helper for the laptop: `lab/espn_clock_poller.py` writes clock, period, score, ball and outs of every live US game every 6 s, so the live rule can be replayed on queue_late's pretend fills (queue_late itself has no game clock).
