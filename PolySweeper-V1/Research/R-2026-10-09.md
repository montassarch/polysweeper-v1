### A. Row 4, 13 months of US games
Data: 6,973 finished games with one resolved moneyline (MLB 2,701, NHL 1,336, NBA 1,188, CFB 990, NFL 377, WNBA 381); 6,285 had a taker sell into a bid at 0.98+ in the last 30 min before Polymarket's stamp (198k fills); ESPN play-by-play matched 98.7%. "Loser" = the bought side lost, or an NFL tie (pays 50/50; earlier studies dropped the 3 NFL ties).
- **Base rate:** the fill lands on the loser in **37 of 6,285 games (0.6%)**: MLB 11/2,564, NBA 7/1,182, NHL 7/1,263, CFB 5/535, WNBA 5/366, NFL 2/375. Below the 1-2 cent margin (positive on average), never zero.
- **Cancel-follow shield (idea 26): rejected.** In 248 of 259 loser fills (96%) the bought side was still AHEAD when it filled; the flip came later. A shield that cancels when the game turns removes at most 4%.
- **"Last 10-12 minutes" (the 32-day guard): does not hold.** 14 loser games have a fill inside the last 12 min before the stamp, 3 inside the last 5 (e.g. Western Michigan at Michigan, 0.999, lead 5 with 7 s left). The 32-day window had none by luck; the stamp is also unknown live.
- **Clock-only rules still lose** (loser games / games, bought side ahead): last 60 s: CFB 1/353, NFL 1/327, NBA 5/1,126, WNBA 3/313; "5 s or less": NFL 1/308 (Denver-Indianapolis, lead 2 at 3 s, last-second field goal), NBA 1/1,019 (lead 3, 2.8 s, overtime), WNBA 1/231. MLB 9th inning or later with a 4+ run lead: 2/730 lost. A one-play "buffer" rule (idea 27) cannot work: loser leads were 2-14 points, goals or runs.
- **Rules with 0 losers:** NBA last 60 s and lead 5+: **0/953 games** (95% bound 0.3%); NBA last 2 min and lead 9+: 0/796; NFL last 60 s and lead 3+: 0/293; hockey rules look clean too (0/672) but are not trustworthy (see traps). And the strict one: **after the game's last play** (ESPN's final row): **0 loser games in 4,263 games, 36,222 fills at 0.98+** (95% bound 0.07%): MLB 0/1,478, NHL 0/1,067, NBA 0/914, NFL 0/334, CFB 0/280, WNBA 0/190; at least 45 s after the last play: 0/2,726 games; cheap band 0.98-0.995 only: 0/1,714 games (15,771 fills), 0/1,061 when 45 s after. A wrong ESPN stamp can only add risk here, so the zero is robust. Not safe: "clock 1 s or less" in football (Montana State-Idaho: lead 6 with under 1 s, Hail Mary touchdown plus extra point). One counter-example below 0.98: Arizona-Texas 13 May, 20 fills at 0.95-0.97 "after" a last row that was a truncated feed (game went on): the live trigger must be ESPN status final, plus a 0.98 price floor.
- **Supply after the last play** (games with 5+ cheap shares at 0.98-0.995; share of all games with a fill): NHL 44% (549 of 1,258), NFL 36%, MLB 24%, NBA 19%, CFB 17%, WNBA 13%; if the bot acts 45 s late: NHL 34%, NFL 30%, CFB 13%, MLB 11%, NBA 10%, WNBA 5%; 90 s late: NHL 24%, NFL 22%, CFB 10%, NBA 9%, MLB 3%. Most cheap fills come BEFORE the last play (40-77% more than 2 min before it); after it: NFL 44%, NHL 42%, MLB 32%, CFB 23%, NBA 11%, WNBA 8%. When supply exists it is deep: median 200 (MLB) to 3,600 (NBA) shares per game. Lead-plus-clock supply: NBA lead 5+ last minute 30% of games, NFL lead 3+ last minute 33%.
- **A resting bid must be cancelled when its rule stops being true.** Price-priority model (5 shares at 0.995, bot starts 45 s after the rule): left alone, NBA "last minute, lead 5+" fills 381 games and 5 are losers; cancelling within 45 s of the rule failing leaves 316 fills and 1 loser.
- **Projection to 21 Oct:** Polymarket already lists CFB 221 games (93 on Sat 10 Oct, 99 on Sat 17), NHL 88, NFL 28, NBA 31 (preseason; season from 20 Oct), MLB 6. Applying the supply shares above: **96 qualifying games (instant), 72 (45 s late), 53 (90 s late)**, so at most about 70 pretend fills even with price priority at 0.995; the laptop's queue_late joins the queue at 0.98/0.99 and has no "after the last play" arm yet. **Row 4 cannot be YES by 21 Oct** (200 needed). Verdict NOT YET, safety promising.
- **Data traps found and fixed:** (1) ESPN's MLB rows carry the score and outs AFTER the whole at-bat on every pitch row (a mid-at-bat read leaks the result; the 32-day MLB gates were slightly leaky); (2) ESPN's NHL clock counts UP, so hockey rules need 1200 minus clock; (3) ESPN play stamps can be 100 s or more off (hockey overtime goal stamped at -526 s while the market still showed 0.91 at -440 s): 5 of 7 hockey losers are "tied in overtime" labels (blown third-period leads), so no hockey clock rule can be trusted from history; (4) NFL ties pay 50/50; (5) Gamma series ids for NFL/CFB change each season (2026: 12185/12756; 2025: 10187/10210); (6) the Data API caps `offset` at 10,000 per window.

### B. New lead: tennis, same method (60 days, 3,305 ATP/WTA singles matches with one resolved moneyline and a finished stamp)
- Cheap supply: in 48% of matches (1,588 of 3,305) takers sold into bids at 0.95-0.995 inside the last 120 s before the stamp, 0 on the loser. The sellers are diffuse retail (1,802 wallets in the last 180 s, top 3 hold 5.5%). Of all sell prints in the last 120 s: 57% hit 0.999+, 29% hit exactly 0.990, only 3.7% 0.991-0.998 (nobody competes for price priority between those levels today).
- Laptop feed race (8-9 Oct): 365Scores / LiveScore say "finished" a median 161 / 157 s before Polymarket's flag (p10 about -3 s, p90 350 s), scores agree 99%. So the last minutes before the stamp are mostly after the real end.
- **Price-priority model** (5-share bid joined T s before the stamp; every later taker sell printed at or below our price would have met us first; nobody above us):

| bid | T = 60 s | 120 s | 180 s | 300 s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.995 (profit $0.025 per fill) | 29% of matches, 0 losers | 43%, 0 losers | 50%, 1 loser | 60%, 1 loser |
| 0.990 (profit $0.05) | 27%, 0 losers | 39%, 0 losers | 46%, 1 loser | 55%, 1 loser |

- Stable: ATP 44% / WTA 40% (T=120); first 30 days 43% / last 30 days 43%. By tier at 0.995, T=120: tour-level 59%, challenger-like 43%, qualifying 38%, Grand Slam main draw 37%, juniors 0%. The one loser fill (Rocha-Harri, 6-7(10-12) super tiebreak, 152 s before the stamp) was still being played.
- **Cross-check with the market's own clock (hindsight, no score feed):** the price "settles" (last print of the winner below 0.98 or of the loser at 0.90 or more) a median 126 s before the stamp (p25 64 s, p75 332 s; 3,055 matches), and 72% of cheap sells in the last 300 s come after that moment. A bid joined at the settling moment fills 61% of matches at 0.995 (56% at 0.99), 30 s later 43% (37%), 60 s later 31% (25%). This agrees with the stamp-based table and does not depend on the laptop's lead figure. The winner's price falls back below 0.95 after first touching 0.98 in 20% of matches, so "price at 0.98" alone is not "over".
- Voids: 120 of 4,143 tennis events paid 50/50, 100 of them "0-0" (never played, walkovers); only 1 void had a cheap fill (480 s before the stamp, a 6-1 6-0 match). Rules text: a retirement pays the player who advances; a walkover or cancelled match pays 50-50 (guard: bid only when set scores exist).
- With the laptop's lead and about 30 s of bot delay the realistic fill rate is **35-45% (up to 60% if the end is spotted as soon as the price settles) of ~50 matches a day = 18-22 pretend fills a day** at $0.025-0.05 each (about $0.5-1 a day); started this weekend it reaches 200+ pretend fills by 21 Oct. Not a result yet: the real queue ahead of a newcomer, other bots pegging above us and the 30 s delay are unmeasured.

**Idea card T1: Tennis "match is over" price-priority bid.** *Mechanism:* two free score sources show the same final score with real set scores; we rest a 5-share buy on the winner just above the best standing bid (cap 0.995; a maker pays no fee) and retail sellers still unloading hit it first; cancel at Polymarket's flag; shares pay $1 at settlement (median 31 min). *Why near-zero loss:* the match is over. *Ways to lose:* wrong or reversed source score (1 disagreement in 45 LiveScore reads; use 2 of 3 sources); walkover or cancelled match pays 50-50 (2.4% of events, almost all never started); source says "finished" for a retirement and Polymarket resolves otherwise (rules say the advancing player wins); bot or data fault; a pegging war eating the margin; an unfilled bid ties up $5. *Per day:* about 18-20 fills, $0.5-1. *Test (no new code):* on the laptop `python3 lab/feeds_to_joins.py 30 > lab/data/raw/joins.jsonl` then `LEAGUE=tennis python3 lab/tennis_decided_bids.py replay lab/data/raw/joins.jsonl` replays every recorded match (join = first source "over" + 30 s) against the real tape; kill if fewer than 20% of 60+ matches fill or any fill lands on a loser. Then add bid-side books (top 5 levels) to the feed race to measure the real queue.

**Idea card R4b: US-games "after the last play" bid with cancel** (replaces idea 29). *Mechanism:* bid 0.98-0.995 on the side that won only once ESPN shows the game final (and, as an option, while NBA last-minute lead 5+ / NFL last-minute lead 3+ holds, cancelling when it stops). *Why near-zero loss:* 0 of 4,263 games after the last play. *Ways to lose:* truncated or wrong ESPN feed (1 game, below 0.98); bid placed under a lead rule that then fails before the cancel (5 vs 1 loser games in the NBA model); football untimed down; NFL tie 50/50; suspended games; no fills (queue). *Per day:* 3-7 qualifying games on busy days, $0.03-0.10 each. *Test:* run `lab/espn_clock_poller.py` beside `queue_late.py`; patch `queue_late.py` to write each token's `polls` into its JSONL line; then simulate the join at "ESPN says final" on the laptop's own books.
